## 5. Conclusion
1. Simulator verified (<=2% per request incl. warm and the 4096 request), so the tables describe what the live engine does on this loop.
2. Warm steady state is NOT compulsory-dominated: 77% of warm reads are capacity re-references at reuse distance 128-512 vs ~259 slots/layer (compulsory ~3% by request 9, 12.5% over warm). Cold request 0 is compulsory + tie-rule (60/40).
3. Realistic headroom vs LRU: -10% (profile-rank tie order, trivial), -11% ARC, -16% LRU-2 (warm); 4096: -9..-15%. That is +0.4..+0.6 tok/s estimated, below measurement noise; ~10 of LRU-2's 16 points are the tie-order fix. Frequency decay adds ~1-2%, 2nd-miss admission is worse and less predictable (worst request +8..+19%), TinyLFU degenerates to LRU. Only Belady OPT (-64%, +2.8 tok/s est.) is far ahead; it needs future knowledge, so it is an oracle bound, not an achievable policy.
4. Recommendation: do not build a new eviction policy as a performance item. The tie-order fix (profile-rank order for never-touched residents, ~10 lines, -10% warm reads, -20% on cold start) is the only cheap item; LRU-2 is the only further step worth considering (+6 points). The remaining traffic is capacity-cyclic rather than compulsory, so the levers are more effective RAM capacity (working set only slightly above 12,448 slots) or future-aware prefetch/profile for the predictable cross-request re-references (oracle worth up to -64% reads). Even that is bounded: only ~0.37 s per GB of decode time depends on these reads, and the fit is weak (R2 0.26-0.39), so promise no more than ~+1-3 tok/s without a live A/B.
